Where it began

Campbell’s story

Growing up, much of my life was shaped by medical uncertainty. I spent countless days in doctors’ offices and hospitals as my family and I searched for answers. Eating enough did not always mean my body received enough; because my body absorbs only a small amount of what I take in, I came to rely on a feeding tube for the nutrition I needed.

Those experiences were difficult, but they also introduced me to extraordinary kindness. My family, friends, medical teams, and people I had never met showed up for me again and again. For a long time, I was focused on getting through the next appointment or the next challenge. I did not fully understand how much support surrounded me—or how many everyday necessities I was fortunate to have.

That perspective changed during my Make-A-Wish trip in 2017. The experience gave me space to look beyond my own circumstances and recognize things I had taken for granted: clean clothes to wear, food on the table, a safe home, and people willing to help whenever life became difficult. I began thinking about families facing the same kinds of uncertainty without those basic layers of support.

On the flight home, I turned to my mom and said, “Mom, I want to start a charity to give back to those in need. So many people have been kind to me, and I want to give back too.” It was not meant to be an idea for someday. My parents believed in it immediately, and our whole family began working together as soon as we landed.

Less than two months later, in October 2017, we received our 501(c)(3) status and Campbell’s Crew Cares officially began. What started as one family’s desire to pay kindness forward grew into a volunteer-powered organization providing food, clothing, school necessities, and essential supplies to people navigating difficult moments.

I am much healthier today than I was during those early years, and I do not want my medical history to be the only thing people see when they hear my story. It is one part of what shaped me. More importantly, it taught me that receiving help can inspire responsibility: when someone gives you hope, you can carry that hope forward and offer it to someone else.
